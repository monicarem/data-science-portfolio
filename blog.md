## Blog 1: Data Science in Social and Interdisciplinary Contexts Matters
When I first started learning data science, I thought it was mostly a technical field. Just coding, statistics, and models. But the more I learn, the more I realize that data science is never just about numbers. It is about people. Every dataset, especially in social contexts, represents real human lives, behaviors, and experiences.

So why model social topics and people? Because so many systems that shape our lives are increasingly driven by data and algorithms. Healthcare, education, criminal justice, housing, employment. If we do not understand how to model these systems thoughtfully, we risk making the same inequalities worse that we are trying to fix. Modeling social topics gives us a way to see patterns that are too large or complex to notice otherwise. But it also means we have to be careful about what those patterns actually represent.

That leads to the question of responsibility. When we use data about people, we have an obligation to be transparent, protect privacy, and think about who benefits and who gets hurt by our work. A model that predicts "risk" in one setting might be used to deny someone a job, a loan, or even their freedom. I am learning that responsibility is not just about accuracy. It is about asking whether a model should exist, who it serves, and what happens when it is wrong.

Through this course, I hope to better understand how to combine technical skill with ethical and social awareness. I want to learn how to ask better questions before I build anything, and how to work with people from other fields like sociology, ethics, and community work. These are perspectives I do not have yet.
